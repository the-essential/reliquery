## Description

Duskhollow earns its name at sunset, when the sandstone architecture catches the dying light and the whole city glows amber for twenty minutes before dropping into blue shadow. The effect is theatrical enough that it feels designed — and knowing the city's history, it probably was.

## History

Founded as a trade waypoint three centuries ago, Duskhollow became a seat of governance by accident: enough factions maintained offices here that the city accumulated political gravity without anyone formally declaring it a capital. By the time someone thought to make it official, it already was.

## Current State

The succession crisis has turned Duskhollow into a pressure cooker. Three factions maintain competing claims to governance, and the city's infrastructure — designed for commerce, not siege politics — is straining under the weight of diplomatic delegations, intelligence operatives, and the quiet buildup of factional muscle.

The streets are calm. The back rooms are not.

## Notable Features

The Amber Forum serves as the nominal seat of governance — a circular amphitheater where the factions maintain the polite fiction of negotiation. The real decisions happen in the warren of private chambers beneath it, accessible by invitation only.

## Notes

Duskhollow functions as a narrative pressure cooker — a confined space where every faction is forced into proximity. The city's beauty is a deliberate counterpoint to the ugliness of the political maneuvering happening inside it. The sunset effect isn't just atmospheric detail; it's a metaphor for the brief, beautiful window before everything goes dark.
